# Filet Mignon with Red Wine Mushroom Sauce

Filet is lean and mild, so the sauce does most of the flavor work.

## Ingredients

**Steak**
- 2 filet mignon steaks
- Kosher salt, black pepper
- High-smoke-point oil (avocado, or tallow)
- 2 tbsp butter
- 1 garlic clove, smashed
- Thyme, optional

**Sauce**
- 8 oz baby bella mushrooms, sliced ¼ inch thick
- 1 yellow onion, diced small
- 2–3 garlic cloves, minced
- ½ cup dry red wine (cabernet, merlot, pinot noir)
- ¾ cup beef broth
- 3 tbsp cold butter, cut into pieces
- 1 tbsp butter for the mushrooms

## 40 minutes before

Salt the filets generously on all sides. Leave uncovered on a plate at room temperature.
The salt pulls moisture out, it reabsorbs, and the surface dries — that dry surface is
what gives you a crust.

Meanwhile: slice the mushrooms thick (thin slices disappear), dice the onion, mince the
garlic and keep it separate, measure the wine and broth, and put the finishing butter back
in the fridge so it stays cold.

Turn on the vent and open a window. This sear will smoke, and that is correct.

## Sear

1. Pat the filets completely dry. Wet meat steams instead of searing.
2. Preheat the empty pan on medium-high, 3–4 minutes. Water drops should **bead up and
   skate**. This is the recipe where Leidenfrost is the target, not the overshoot.
3. Add about a tablespoon of oil. It should shimmer and just barely wisp. Put the steaks
   down promptly once it does — do not let a thin film sit and burn off.
4. Lay the filets down and **do not touch them for 3 minutes.**
5. Flip. 2–3 more minutes.
6. Hold each filet on its edge with tongs, 30 seconds per side, to sear the rim.
7. Drop to medium-low. Add 2 tbsp butter, the smashed garlic, thyme if using. Tilt the pan
   and spoon the foaming butter over the tops for 1–2 minutes.
8. Pull at **120–125°F** internal for medium-rare. Rest loosely tented **8–10 minutes.**
   It will climb another 5 degrees.

For filets over 2 inches thick: skip the basting and move the pan to a 400°F oven for
4–6 minutes after step 6. Remember the handle will be dangerously hot and look identical
to a cold one.

## Sauce, while the steak rests

1. Pour off all but a thin film of fat. **Take the pan off the burner for a minute** — it is
   far too hot for mushrooms right now.
2. Back on medium. 1 tbsp butter, then mushrooms in a single layer. Leave them 3–4 minutes.
   They release water; wait it out until the liquid cooks off and they start browning.
   **Salt now, not before.**
3. Add the onion. 3–4 minutes until soft.
4. Add the garlic. 30 seconds only.
5. Pour in the wine. Scrape up the fond while it bubbles. **Reduce until nearly dry,
   3–4 minutes.** Do not rush this — under-reduced wine tastes sharp and boozy.
6. Add the broth. Simmer until reduced by half and it coats a spoon, about 5 minutes.
7. **Off the heat**, swirl in the cold butter one piece at a time. Off the heat matters —
   boiling butter breaks the sauce.
8. Add any juices pooled under the resting steaks. Taste for salt.

Spoon the sauce over the rested filets, or pool it underneath so the crust stays crisp.

## Notes

- If it reads acidic, a small pinch of sugar or one more knob of butter rounds it out.
- Dijon, Worcestershire, or a splash of cream all deepen it.
- Avocado spray works for the sear but pouring gives better coverage. Bottled oil or tallow
  is the upgrade.
- Watch for oil smoking during preheat. If it smokes, wipe the pan and start with fresh fat
  rather than proceeding.
