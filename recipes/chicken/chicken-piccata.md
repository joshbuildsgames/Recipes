# Chicken Piccata

Adapted from the NYT Cooking version. The changes below are all about making it work on
stainless without scorching the butter.

## Ingredients

- Chicken cutlets, pounded thin
- Kosher salt, black pepper
- All-purpose flour, for dredging
- Light or refined olive oil (not extra-virgin — see notes)
- Butter, 3–4 tbsp, mostly reserved for the sauce
- 1 shallot, sliced
- 1 lemon, sliced thin, plus juice
- Dry white wine
- Chicken broth
- Capers
- Parsley

## Method

1. Season the cutlets with salt and pepper. Pat dry. Dredge in flour and shake off the
   excess — **dredge right before the chicken hits the pan** so the flour does not turn pasty.

2. Preheat the pan on **medium**, 2–3 minutes. A water drop should **sizzle hard and vanish
   in a second or two** — roughly 300°F. It should *not* bead and skate. That is a steak
   temperature and will smoke the oil before the chicken goes in.

3. Add oil. When it just barely shimmers, go. Smoke means the window was missed — take the
   pan off the burner, wipe it, start again.

4. Lay the cutlets down without crowding the pan. Crowding creates steam and prevents
   browning. **Leave them undisturbed 3–4 minutes.** Floured chicken grabs at first and
   releases once the crust forms. If it will not budge, give it another 30 seconds.

5. Flip, cook through, remove to a plate. Repeat with the remaining cutlets, adding fresh
   oil as needed.

6. Between batches: if the fond is golden brown, leave it — that is the sauce. If any spots
   have gone black, wipe the pan and add fresh oil, or the bitterness carries into the sauce.

7. Sauté the shallot and lemon slices until lightly caramelized.

8. Deglaze with white wine, scraping up the fond. Add broth and lemon juice, simmer until
   reduced by about half.

9. Add capers. **Off the heat**, swirl in the cold butter a piece at a time. If it boils hard
   the sauce breaks and goes greasy.

10. Return the chicken to coat, finish with parsley.

## Notes

- **The original calls for medium-high heat with butter and olive oil.** On stainless that
  runs hot. Medium is the right setting, and most or all of the butter belongs in the sauce
  rather than the sear.
- Extra-virgin olive oil smokes at 350–400°F, right at the edge of the working window. Use
  light or refined olive oil for the cooking and save the extra-virgin for finishing.
- Ghee is the best single-fat option here: butter flavor, 480°F smoke point.
- Stainless is non-reactive, so the lemon and wine are no problem — unlike cast iron.

## What went wrong the first time

Used the bead-and-skate water test, which put the pan near 400°F. The olive oil smoked
immediately and the butter browned the instant it hit. Turning the burner down did not help
fast enough because clad pans hold heat.

The fix: sizzle-and-vanish, not bead-and-skate. And lift the pan off the burner to cool it
rather than relying on the dial.
