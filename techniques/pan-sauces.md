# Pan Sauces

## The universal sequence

Nearly every pan sauce follows the same five moves. Swap the liquid and the aromatics and
you have a different dish with the same bones.

1. **Sear** the protein, remove it, let it rest
2. **Sweat** aromatics in the residual fat
3. **Deglaze** with wine or spirits, reduce until nearly dry
4. **Reduce** with stock until it coats a spoon
5. **Mount** with cold butter, off the heat

This works for steak, pork chops, chicken thighs, fish — anything that leaves fond.

## Fond does not require meat

Fond is sugars and proteins browning against hot metal. Mushrooms, onions, tomato paste,
and flour all produce it. Which means the entire sauce skill set can be practiced in
15 minutes with no protein at all.

## Critical failure points

**Mounting butter over heat.** The emulsion breaks and the sauce goes greasy. Pull the pan
off the burner and swirl in *cold* butter one piece at a time. Cold matters — it melts
slowly enough to emulsify rather than separate.

**Under-reduced wine.** Wine that has not cooked down to near-dry tastes sharp and boozy in
the finished sauce. Give it the full 3–4 minutes.

**A pan that is still steak-hot.** After a hard sear the pan is far too hot for mushrooms or
aromatics. Take it off the burner for a minute before starting the sauce.

**Salting mushrooms too early.** Salt draws water out and they steam instead of browning.
Salt after they have started to brown.

## Fixes

- **Broken sauce:** off heat, add an ice cube or a splash of cold cream, whisk hard.
- **Too acidic:** a small pinch of sugar, or one more knob of butter.
- **Too thin:** keep reducing. It should coat the back of a spoon and hold a line when you
  draw a finger through it (nappe).
- **Too salty:** more unsalted stock, then reduce again.

Cream is far harder to break than butter. Finishing with cream instead is the low-risk
version of the same sauce.

# Practice course

Seven units, each one weeknight, none requiring you to cook a protein first. Output goes
over pasta, rice, eggs, or toast.

## Unit 1 — Emulsification, no fond
**Beurre blanc.** Shallot, white wine and vinegar, reduce nearly dry, then cold butter off
the heat piece by piece. Pure temperature control with nothing to hide behind. Repeat three
or four times until you can feel when the pan is too hot.

## Unit 2 — Break it deliberately, then fix it
Same sauce, but let it boil. Watch it separate. Then recover: off heat, ice cube or cold
cream, whisk hard. Learning the rescue removes the fear that causes undercooked sauces.

## Unit 3 — Fond from vegetables
Mushrooms alone, browned hard until there is real brown stuff on the pan. Deglaze, reduce,
mount with butter. Proves the fond principle with no steak in sight.

## Unit 4 — Tomato paste fond (pinçage)
Cook tomato paste in fat until it darkens to brick red and sticks to the pan. The fastest
and most dramatic fond available. Deglaze with wine or broth. This one changes how you think
about sauce depth.

## Unit 5 — Reduction and nappe
Plain broth. Reduce by half, then by three quarters. Taste at each stage. Learn what "coats
the back of a spoon" actually looks like so you stop guessing.

## Unit 6 — Roux and the stable sauces
Butter and flour, then milk or broth. Velouté and béchamel. Forgiving, they can boil, and
they are the fallback when an emulsion is not worth the risk.

## Unit 7 — Cream as insurance
The same pan sauce finished with cream instead of butter. Understanding why cream resists
breaking teaches you what the butter emulsion is actually doing.

By Unit 7 you should be able to walk up to any protein and any deglazing liquid and improvise.
